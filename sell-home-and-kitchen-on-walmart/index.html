To sell home and kitchen products on Walmart, you need a Walmart Marketplace seller account and new items in retail packaging. Most of the category isn't gated, and the referral fee is 15% for Home, Kitchen, Decor & Garden, with different tiers for compact appliances and furniture. The bigger challenges are size, breakage and Walmart's own low prices. Restrictions in home and kitchen

Most home goods don't need pre-approval, but a few rules apply:

  • PFAS in cookware. Walmart's PFAS policy allows cookware with intentionally added PFAS only if it's disclosed on the label and listing, and restricts covered products in states that ban them. Don't write "PFAS-free" in titles or images; "Free of Intentionally Added PFAS" needs a manufacturer certificate of conformance.
  • State-restricted products. Walmart's restricted products policy names furniture among items that can't be sold in every state, so check the product's compliance.
  • Food-contact and Prop 65. Other prohibited-products policies, including Proposition 65, still apply.
  • Brands. Premium kitchen brands may enforce authorized sellers; see Walmart restricted brands.

What sells and seasonality

Home and kitchen has steady everyday demand: storage and organization, small appliances, cookware, bakeware, bedding and bath. Seasonal peaks include January organizing, spring cleaning and patio and garden, back-to-college dorm items in late summer, and Q4 for small appliances and gifts. Holiday decor sells in a short window, then drops hard.

Sourcing home and kitchen, and the risks

Retail and clearance: seasonal decor and discontinued kitchen lines are common clearance finds; see Walmart clearance reselling. Online arbitrage: works for lighter items where shipping doesn't wipe out margin; see online arbitrage on Walmart. Wholesale: good for repeatable staples; see Walmart wholesale.

  • Size and weight. Bulky items cost more to ship and store. Fee math that works for a spatula fails for a step stool.
  • Fragile items. Glass, ceramics and small appliances break in transit; returns are expensive.
  • Walmart and its private labels compete on price across home basics.
  • Variations. Colors and sizes can share a listing but sell very differently; check the specific variation.
  • Seasonal decor that misses its window can sit for a year.

Checklist before you buy home and kitchen items

  1. Weigh and measure it, and price fulfillment for that size.
  2. Confirm the fee category (home, compact appliance or furniture).
  3. Check PFAS and state restrictions for cookware and furniture.
  4. Check sellers, stock and Walmart's offer, and the variation you'd sell.
  5. Run profit and check price history for seasonal swings.

WFS notes for home and kitchen

WFS fees rise with size and weight, so run WFS fees before sending bulky goods. Fragile items must come in a six-sided rigid box, and Walmart recommends lab testing fragile packaging. Factor in storage months for slow, seasonal items.

Questions

What is Walmart's referral fee for home and kitchen?

Home, Kitchen, Decor & Garden is 15%. Compact appliances are 12% up to $300 and 8% above, major appliances are 8%, and furniture is 15% up to $200 and 10% above.

Do home and kitchen products need approval on Walmart?

Most don't. Watch for PFAS disclosure rules on cookware, state-restricted products such as some furniture, and brands that restrict their resellers.

Can I sell nonstick cookware on Walmart?

Cookware with intentionally added PFAS is allowed only with disclosure on the label and listing, and is restricted in states that ban it. Don't claim PFAS-free without a manufacturer certificate.

Are bulky home items worth selling on Walmart?

Sometimes, but shipping and WFS fees rise with size and weight. Run profit with real fulfillment costs before buying.
