What a retail arbitrage app should show

Before you put an item in your cart, the app should answer five questions:

  1. What does it sell for? The current price and Buy Box price on the marketplace, not the list price.
  2. What do fees take? The referral fee for the category plus fulfillment (WFS on Walmart, FBA on Amazon).
  3. What do I keep? Profit and ROI after your cost, and the most you can pay and still hit your target.
  4. Will it sell? A sales estimate. Walmart has no public sales rank, so a Walmart app has to estimate from other signals and should say when there isn't enough data.
  5. Who am I up against? Every competing seller, their stock, and who usually wins the Buy Box.

If an app only shows price and a rough profit, you'll still be guessing on the two things that sink most deals: slow sales and a crowded listing.

Why Amazon apps give wrong numbers for Walmart

Most retail arbitrage apps were built for Amazon. They apply Amazon's fee tables and Amazon's sales rank. Walmart Marketplace has its own referral fees by category, its own WFS fulfillment fees, no public sales rank and a different Buy Box. Running a Walmart deal through an Amazon app can make a loser look like a winner. That's why we built a Walmart arbitrage app instead of adding Walmart to an Amazon tool.

Free vs paid retail arbitrage apps

The Amazon Seller app is free for registered Amazon sellers, and some third-party apps have free tiers with scan limits. Store apps like the Walmart app have a free price scanner, but it shows the store price only, not seller fees, profit or sales. Paid apps add the data that stops bad buys. Questions

What is the best retail arbitrage app?

It depends on where you sell. For Walmart Marketplace, Margenos is built for Walmart's fees, sales estimates, sellers and Buy Box. For Amazon, resellers commonly use the Amazon Seller app, SellerAmp SAS, Scoutify 2, Profit Bandit or Scan Profit. Pick the app made for your marketplace first.

Is there a free retail arbitrage app?

The Amazon Seller app is free for registered Amazon sellers, and some apps have free tiers with scan limits. The Walmart app's price scanner is free but shows only the store price. Margenos, for Walmart Marketplace, is paid with a 14-day free trial.

What app do resellers use to check prices?

Most resellers use a scanner app made for the marketplace they sell on. Amazon sellers often use the Amazon Seller app or SellerAmp SAS. Walmart Marketplace sellers use a Walmart tool such as Margenos, which shows Walmart fees, profit, sales estimates and competing sellers from a barcode scan.

Can I use an Amazon retail arbitrage app for Walmart?

Not reliably. Amazon apps use Amazon's fees and sales rank. Walmart has different referral and WFS fees and no public sales rank, so the profit an Amazon app shows won't match what you keep on Walmart.

Is there a retail arbitrage app for iPhone?

Yes for Amazon: the Amazon Seller app and several third-party scanners run on iPhone. For Walmart, the Margenos app is on Android now and the iPhone app is coming soon. Until then, iPhone users can use the Margenos Chrome extension on a computer.

Do I need a Walmart seller account to use Margenos?

No. Margenos works without a Walmart seller account, so you can research deals before or while your application is reviewed.

Is retail arbitrage legal?

Buying products at retail and reselling them is generally legal in the U.S., but brands and marketplaces can restrict who sells certain items. This is general information, not legal advice. See our guide on whether retail arbitrage is legal.
