Walmart competitor analysis: what to look at

Once a product looks profitable, the next question is who you'd be selling against. A useful Walmart competitor analysis covers:

  • Number of sellers on the listing, and how many are actively winning sales.
  • Who wins the Buy Box and how often. If Walmart holds it most days, your share will be small. See how the Walmart Buy Box works.
  • Fulfillment. Which offers use WFS and which ship themselves. WFS offers can win at slightly higher prices.
  • Stock. Units left per seller and total stock across sellers, compared with sales. The stock checker page shows how to read it.
  • Price trend. Whether the Buy Box price is holding or sliding.

Margenos shows all five on one screen. If you already sell an item, Walmart's Search Insights dashboard in Seller Center also lets you compare your item's performance with similar products in the same product type.

A Walmart product research checklist

  1. Find the listing by barcode, name or Walmart.com page, and make sure it's the right variation or pack size.
  2. Enter your cost and prep to get profit, ROI and max buy cost after referral and WFS fees.
  3. Read the sales estimate and its source. Treat "not enough data" and slow-seller warnings as reasons to buy fewer or pass. More in the Walmart sales estimator.
  4. Run the competitor checks above.
  5. Check whether the brand or category needs Walmart's approval before you buy.
  6. Buy a test quantity, then track the item for price and seller alerts.
