How to sell toys on Walmart

Fees, safety paperwork, the holiday rush and how resellers check a toy before they buy it.

To sell toys on Walmart, you need an approved Walmart Marketplace seller account, a toy that's new in its retail packaging, and the safety paperwork Walmart requires for children's products: a Children's Product Certificate (CPC) and a third-party lab test report. Walmart charges a 15% referral fee on Toys & Games. Walmart's referral fee for toys

Walmart's published schedule lists Toys & Games at 15% of the total sales price, with no price tiers. The total sales price includes the item price plus any shipping, gift wrap and other charges, and the fee is only charged after a sale. There's no setup or monthly fee for Marketplace sellers.

Hypothetical example: a building set sells for $29.99 with free shipping. The referral fee is 15% × $29.99 = $4.50. If you charged $5.99 shipping instead, the fee would be 15% × $35.98 = $5.40. WFS fulfillment fees, if you use WFS, come on top. See all Walmart seller fees for how this fits with other costs.

Do toys need approval on Walmart?

Toys aren't on Walmart's general pre-approval list, but children's products carry their own requirements. Walmart's children's products policy covers items designed or intended primarily for children 12 and under, and says each one must be accompanied by a valid CPC and a test report from a CPSC-accepted third-party lab, both in English. Walmart can ask you to upload them through the Item compliance widget on the Health and Compliance page in Seller Center, and failing to provide them when asked is prohibited.

That matters for resellers. A retail receipt doesn't come with a CPC. Big-brand toys usually have one from the manufacturer, but you may need to get it from the brand or distributor if Walmart asks. Two more rules to know:

  • Halloween and select seasonal products are on Walmart's pre-approval list, so costumes and seasonal novelties can be gated.
  • Brand enforcement applies here too. Licensed character toys can draw IP claims. Read Walmart restricted brands before buying deep on a licensed line.

What toys sell and when

Toys are one of the most seasonal categories on any marketplace. Demand builds from October through December, and many shoppers buy toys as gifts, so the fourth quarter is when most toy resellers make their year. Outside Q4, birthdays and summer outdoor play keep steady sellers moving: building sets, card and board games, plush, arts and crafts, and outdoor toys like water blasters in spring.

The flip side is January. Prices on hot holiday toys often fall after the season as sellers dump stock. Watch a listing's price and Buy Box history (Margenos keeps its own daily snapshots, since Walmart.com doesn't publish history) to see how a toy behaved last year.

Sourcing toys and the risks

Retail and clearance. Post-holiday and end-of-season toy clearance is a classic reseller source; see Walmart clearance reselling.

Online arbitrage. Retailer sales on popular sets can work, but margins are thin at 15%. See online arbitrage on Walmart.

Wholesale. Buying from distributors gives you invoices and an easier path to CPCs; see Walmart wholesale.

Category-specific risks:

  • Walmart sells many toys itself. When Walmart is in stock at a low price, third-party sellers often struggle to win the Buy Box.
  • Counterfeits. Popular collectible and character toys are copied. Buy from sources you trust and keep invoices.
  • Crushed boxes. Collectors reject damaged packaging, and returns eat profit.
  • Batteries. Toys with lithium batteries face shipping rules; Walmart's hazardous items policy bans loose 18650 cells but allows them installed in a device.

Checklist before you buy a toy to resell

  1. Confirm a CPC exists or that you can get one from the brand or distributor.
  2. Check who's selling. Is Walmart on the listing? How many sellers, and how much stock?
  3. Check the sales estimate and whether it's from Q4 or a normal month.
  4. Run profit at a realistic price, not today's spike, after the 15% fee and fulfillment.
  5. Check the price history for last season's post-holiday drop.

WFS notes for toys

Walmart Fulfillment Services can help in Q4 because Walmart stores, picks and ships the orders during the busiest weeks. Plan inbound shipments early, because late holiday stock that doesn't sell turns into storage fees. Packaging rules matter: WFS requires fragile items in a six-sided rigid box, and packaged batteries must be separated to prevent short circuits. Check costs on Walmart WFS fees.

Questions

What is Walmart's referral fee for toys?

Walmart's published schedule lists Toys & Games at 15% of the total sales price, which includes shipping and other charges. It's only charged when the item sells.

Do I need approval to sell toys on Walmart?

Toys aren't on Walmart's general pre-approval list, but children's products need a valid Children's Product Certificate and a third-party lab test report. Halloween and select seasonal products do need pre-approval.

Can I resell toys bought at retail on Walmart?

Yes, if they're new in retail packaging and not from a brand that restricts resellers. You still need to be able to provide a CPC and test report if Walmart asks, which can be hard to get without a distributor relationship.

When do toys sell best on Walmart?

Demand peaks in the fourth quarter around the holidays. Many toys drop in price after the season, so check the price history before buying to hold.

How do I know if a toy will be profitable on Walmart?

Check the profit after the 15% fee and fulfillment, the sales estimate, how many sellers and how much stock are on the listing, and whether Walmart sells it. Margenos shows those numbers on the Walmart.com listing.

Can I sell LEGO on Walmart Marketplace?

Possibly, but check your own account first. Walmart doesn't publish a list of individual restricted brands, so test-list a LEGO set in Seller Center (or match the existing listing) and see whether it publishes, and check Account, Health and Compliance for restricted categories you need approval for. Sets must be new and sealed, and keep invoices in case Walmart or the brand asks; see Walmart restricted brands before buying in bulk, including from clearance.
