Online arbitrage is buying products from retail websites, usually on sale, clearance or with coupons and cashback, and reselling them on a marketplace like Walmart.com for more than you paid after fees. On Walmart Marketplace it's allowed when you sell new, genuine products you own, ship them yourself or through Walmart Fulfillment Services (WFS), and never have the store you bought from ship straight to your customer. What is online arbitrage?

Arbitrage means profiting from a price gap between two markets. In online arbitrage (often shortened to OA), the first market is a retail website such as a department store, pharmacy or big-box store's site. The second is the marketplace where you resell. You buy at the low price, have the items shipped to you or a prep center, then list and sell them.

It's the same math as retail arbitrage, minus the driving. You can check hundreds of deals from a laptop, which makes it easier to scale. The trade-off is competition: a good online deal is visible to every reseller with the same software, so margins can disappear within hours.

How Walmart online arbitrage works

Walmart online arbitrage means sourcing online and selling on Walmart Marketplace. You need an approved Walmart seller account (see how to sell on Walmart), and you choose how orders ship:

  • Seller-fulfilled: the items come to you, and you ship each order in your own packaging.
  • WFS: you send inventory to Walmart's warehouses and Walmart picks, packs and ships. WFS charges a fulfillment fee per unit by shipping weight and monthly storage. See the full WFS fee chart.

Either way, every sale pays a Walmart referral fee. Walmart says 15% is its most common rate, but it varies by category: consumer electronics is 8%, for example. Walmart charges no setup or monthly fee.

Walmart's rules for online arbitrage

Walmart doesn't ban online arbitrage, but several policies shape how you can do it. These are the ones that matter most:

  • No retailer-to-customer shipping. Walmart's shipping policy says you may not buy products from another retailer and have that retailer ship the order directly to your customer. Buying online and having it shipped to you or your prep center is how compliant OA works.
  • No competitor packaging or branding. You can't ship orders in a competitor's boxes, and the packing slip, invoice and outer packaging can't carry the name of anyone except the manufacturer or you, the seller.
  • Ownership and brand rights. Walmart's prohibited products policy says you must own the items you sell and have the licensing rights to sell branded products. It also prohibits reselling certain products from other online marketplaces, so sourcing from a site like eBay is riskier than buying from a retailer.
  • New condition only. Unless Walmart has invited you to its Resold program, you can list only new items.
  • Pre-approval categories. Some categories, including books, fragrances, luxury brands, software and ingestible and topical products, need Walmart's approval first. Read Walmart restricted brands and categories before you buy.

Keep your receipts and order confirmations. If a brand or Walmart questions where an item came from, your purchase records are your evidence.

Step-by-step online arbitrage workflow for Walmart

  1. Find a lead. Browse retailer sale and clearance pages, deal newsletters or a lead tool. Note the item's UPC if the retailer shows it.
  2. Match it to the right Walmart listing. Search Walmart.com by UPC or exact title, and confirm size, count and variation match. A wrong match is the most expensive mistake in OA.
  3. Check the numbers. With the Margenos Chrome extension open on the Walmart listing, type in your cost. You see referral and WFS fees, profit, ROI and the most you can pay for your target ROI.
  4. Check demand and competition. Look at the sales estimate, how many sellers are on the listing, their stock, whether Walmart itself sells it, and who holds the Buy Box.
  5. Check restrictions. Make sure the brand and category aren't gated for your account before you order.
  6. Buy and ship to yourself or a prep center. Stack any coupons, gift card discounts or cashback, and keep the order confirmation.
  7. Prep and list. Inspect items, label them, and either send them to WFS or ship orders yourself in neutral packaging.
  8. Track and reprice. Watch the listing for new sellers and price drops. Margenos can alert you on tracked products when prices move or sellers join or leave.

What to check before every online arbitrage buy

Your real cost is more than the checkout price. Add sales tax (unless you have a resale certificate the retailer accepts), shipping to you, prep supplies, and shipping to WFS if you use it. Then compare that to profit after Walmart's fees, not the price gap. Watch the WFS under-$10 surcharge: items priced below $10 pay an extra $1 per unit. Our guide to calculating Walmart profit walks through the full formula.

Finally, ask how many units the listing can absorb. Ten units on a listing that sells a handful a month across five sellers can sit for a long time, and WFS storage keeps charging while it sits.

Questions

What is online arbitrage?

Online arbitrage is buying products from retail websites at a discount and reselling them on a marketplace such as Walmart.com, Amazon or eBay for more than your total cost after fees.

Is online arbitrage legal on Walmart?

Yes, selling genuine, new products you bought legally is allowed, as long as you follow Walmart's policies. You can't have the retailer ship directly to your customer, you can't use competitor packaging, and you need Walmart's approval for pre-approval categories.

Can I dropship from another retailer to a Walmart customer?

No. Walmart's shipping and fulfillment policy says you may not buy products from another retailer and have that retailer ship the order directly to the customer. Have items shipped to you or a prep center first.

What are the best online arbitrage tools for Walmart?

Most online arbitrage tools, such as Tactical Arbitrage and Keepa, are built for Amazon. For the Walmart side of the deal you need a tool that reads Walmart listings: Margenos shows fees, profit, ROI, sales estimates and every competing seller on any Walmart.com listing, and WAMP from SellerAmp is another Walmart option.

Is there an online arbitrage tool like Tactical Arbitrage for Walmart?

Tactical Arbitrage matches retail deals to Amazon, not Walmart. Nepeto lists an Amazon-to-Walmart feature. Margenos doesn't scan retail sites for leads; it checks whether a Walmart listing is worth selling once you have a lead.

How much money do you need to start online arbitrage on Walmart?

There's no fixed amount. Walmart charges no monthly fee, so your main costs are inventory, shipping and any software. Many sellers start with small test buys of a few units per product to learn which listings actually sell.

Is online arbitrage still profitable?

It can be, deal by deal. Popular online deals attract many resellers, so the margin depends on checking fees, sales speed and competition before you buy, and on not overbuying.
