Walmart third-party sellers: are they legit?

How to spot a Marketplace seller on Walmart.com, what protects you as a buyer, and what the other sellers on a listing mean if you resell.

Yes, Walmart has third-party sellers. A large share of the products on Walmart.com are sold by independent businesses through Walmart Marketplace, not by Walmart itself. Most of them are legit: every seller has to apply and be approved, Walmart tracks their performance, and Walmart's return policy covers Marketplace orders. But the quality varies from seller to seller, so it's worth checking who you're buying from. Here's how to tell, what protects you, and why it matters if you resell.

Does Walmart have third-party sellers?

It does, and it has for a long time. Walmart's online marketplace launched in 2009 and lets outside businesses list products on Walmart.com next to Walmart's own inventory. Walmart allows third-party sellers because they add millions of products it doesn't have to buy or store, and Walmart earns a referral fee on each sale. For a full explainer, see what is Walmart Marketplace.

How to tell if a Walmart seller is third-party

Look near the price and "Add to cart" button on the product page:

  • "Sold and shipped by Walmart.com" means Walmart is the seller.
  • "Sold and shipped by [company name]" means a third-party seller sells and ships it.
  • "Sold by [company name]" and fulfilled by Walmart means a third-party seller owns the item, but it ships from Walmart's warehouses through Walmart Fulfillment Services.
  • Other sellers or more seller options means several sellers offer the same item; you can compare their prices.

Click the seller's name to see their seller page, rating and reviews.

Are Walmart third-party sellers legit?

Most are real businesses, and Walmart filters them harder than many marketplaces:

  • Application-only. Sellers need a registered business with a U.S. tax ID and must be approved by Walmart. There's no open sign-up.
  • Performance standards. Walmart measures on-time delivery, cancellations and customer response, and can suppress, suspend or terminate sellers who fall short.
  • Pro Seller badge. Sellers who hit Walmart's highest targets, including 95% on-time delivery and a 4.0+ seller rating, get a badge next to their name. See what a Walmart Pro Seller is.

That said, "approved" isn't the same as "perfect." Counterfeits and slow shippers do show up on every big marketplace.

Can you trust Walmart sellers? A quick checklist

  • Check the seller rating and read the most recent reviews, not just the star average.
  • Be careful with a brand-new seller that has no reviews, especially on popular brand-name items.
  • Watch the delivery estimate. A very long one can mean the item ships from overseas.
  • Compare the price with other sellers on the listing and other stores. A price far below everyone else on a hot item is a red flag.
  • Look for the Pro Seller badge or "fulfilled by Walmart" when you want extra confidence.

Buyer protection on Walmart Marketplace orders

Walmart's Marketplace returns policy, published in its seller guides, gives shoppers real protection:

  • 30-day minimum return window for most items, which starts seven days after the item ships. Sellers can offer longer. Some items, like major appliances and certain luxury goods, have different rules or are final sale.
  • Sellers must accept eligible returns regardless of the reason, and can't stall or block them.
  • In-store returns are accepted for eligible Marketplace items, based on the seller's return settings. Big, heavy, freight and some other items are excluded.
  • Walmart can step in. If a seller doesn't follow the return policy, Walmart reserves the right to refund the customer on the seller's behalf.

If a seller doesn't respond, contact Walmart Customer Care through the order in your Walmart.com account.

How many sellers are on Walmart Marketplace?

Walmart rarely publishes the number. Marketplace Pulse reported about 150,000 active sellers in September 2024, up from around 50,000 as recently as 2022, with roughly 35% based outside the U.S., nearly all in China. For comparison, Amazon has far more sellers, which is part of why Walmart listings often have less competition.

Are Walmart sellers reliable compared with Amazon sellers?

Both marketplaces have great sellers and bad ones. The main difference is the front door: Walmart approves each seller by application, while Amazon lets most sellers sign up and starts screening from there. Walmart also has far fewer sellers overall. Neither fact guarantees a good order, so the same habits apply on both sites: check the seller, check the reviews, and know the return policy before you buy. Why third-party sellers matter if you resell

If you're a reseller, the shopper's question flips around. You're the third-party seller, and the other third-party sellers on a listing are your competition. Before you buy inventory, you want to know:

  • How many sellers are on the listing, and how much stock each has.
  • Whether Walmart itself sells it. Competing with Walmart for the Buy Box is hard.
  • Who ships how: WFS or seller-fulfilled, since that affects who wins the Buy Box.
  • Whether prices are dropping as more sellers pile in.

Questions

Does Walmart have third-party sellers?

Yes. Walmart Marketplace lets approved independent businesses sell on Walmart.com alongside Walmart's own inventory. The product page shows who the seller is.

Are Walmart third-party sellers legit?

Most are. Every seller must be an approved, registered business, Walmart tracks their performance, and Marketplace orders are covered by Walmart's return policy. Still check the seller's rating and reviews.

How do I know if I'm buying from Walmart or a third-party seller?

Look for "Sold and shipped by" near the price. If it says Walmart.com, Walmart is the seller; any other name is a Marketplace seller.

Can I return a third-party seller item to a Walmart store?

Often, yes. Walmart accepts eligible Marketplace items in store based on the seller's return settings. Large, heavy, freight and some other items are excluded.

What if a Walmart Marketplace seller won't refund me?

Contact Walmart Customer Care through your order. Walmart reserves the right to refund customers on the seller's behalf when a seller doesn't follow the return policy.

How many sellers are on Walmart Marketplace?

Walmart doesn't publish an official count. Marketplace Pulse reported about 150,000 active sellers in September 2024.

Why does Walmart allow third-party sellers?

They add a huge range of products Walmart doesn't have to buy or store, and Walmart earns a referral fee on each sale.

Why is Walmart Marketplace more expensive than Walmart?

Because Marketplace sellers set their own prices, and they have to cover their product cost, Walmart's referral fee and shipping. The same item can cost more from a third-party seller than when Walmart sells it, especially when Walmart is out of stock. Walmart does limit extreme pricing: its pricing rules can unpublish offers priced well above recent prices, but compare sellers on the listing before you buy.

Is it safe to buy from a Walmart seller with no reviews?

Often it is, because every new seller starts with zero reviews and Walmart approves sellers before they can list, but it carries more risk than buying from an established seller. Check the delivery estimate, compare the price with other sellers on the listing, and be wary of big discounts on popular brand-name items. Your order is still covered by the Walmart Marketplace Promise, so Walmart can step in if the seller doesn't deliver or refund.

What is the Walmart Marketplace Promise?

It's Walmart's guarantee for items sold and shipped by third-party sellers. Per Walmart's help page, it covers problems like late or missing deliveries, items shipped to the wrong address and returns the seller refuses or doesn't refund: the seller gets two business days to resolve it, and if you're still not satisfied, Walmart steps in. Refund requests are accepted for 30 days after the last estimated delivery date.

Can Walmart sellers scam you?

It's possible but uncommon, and you're protected when you stay on Walmart.com. All Marketplace orders go through Walmart's checkout, Walmart doesn't share your payment details with the seller, and the Walmart Marketplace Promise lets Walmart step in on missing items or refused refunds. The real risks are counterfeits and sellers asking you to pay or communicate off Walmart.com, so never do that, and report sellers who break the rules.
