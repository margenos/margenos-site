Walmart Brand Portal: what it does and what resellers should know

Who can enroll, what brand owners can do with it, and how Walmart handles the IP complaints that follow.

Walmart Brand Portal is Walmart's tool for brand owners to report intellectual property (IP) infringement on Walmart.com and to give Marketplace sellers brand privileges, such as authorized reseller status. Only rights owners with an active registered trademark can enroll; resellers can't apply on their own. For resellers, Brand Portal matters because it's where most Walmart IP complaints come from, and repeated valid complaints can cost you listings or your account.

What Walmart Brand Portal does

According to Walmart's Brand Portal overview (updated Nov 6, 2026), once a brand is approved, the rights owner can:

  • Submit IP claims for copyright, trademark, patent and counterfeit infringement.
  • Assign seller roles: make a Marketplace seller an acting brand owner or an authorized reseller. Walmart says this typically takes effect within 24 hours.
  • Invite users to submit IP claims for the brand.
  • Authorize a Brand Protection Service Provider to act for the brand.

Those roles change who controls a listing. An acting brand owner can update item content over any other seller, including authorized resellers, and can set up a brand shop. An authorized reseller can do the same, except over the acting brand owner.

Walmart brand registry: who can enroll

People often search for a "Walmart brand registry," borrowing Amazon's term. On Walmart, Brand Portal is the equivalent. Walmart's eligibility rules:

RequirementWhat Walmart says
Who appliesThe rights owner. Third-party sellers and Brand Protection Service Providers can't apply directly; the rights owner authorizes them.
Where you're basedUnited States, United Kingdom, Canada, China, Hong Kong, India, South Korea or Vietnam
TrademarkAn active registration with a seven-digit registration number. Walmart notes that an eight-digit serial number may mean the mark is still pending and not yet eligible.
AccountWalmart's registration guide says to sign in with your existing Seller Center credentials, then enter your trademark number and brand name exactly as registered.
ReviewWalmart reviews each application; you track its status in Brand Portal. No timeframe is published.

Walmart's guides don't list a fee for Brand Portal. The registration steps are in Register your brand on Walmart Marketplace (updated Mar 30, 2026), and the portal itself is at brandportal.walmart.com.

How Walmart IP complaints work

Walmart's IP infringement claims page explains what happens after a rights owner files through Brand Portal or the online IP Claim Form:

  • The claimant swears to it. They declare, under penalty of perjury, that the information is correct and that they own the rights or act for the owner.
  • Trademark and patent claims are forwarded to the seller before Walmart takes action. Walmart says it doesn't mediate these disputes but can remove infringing content.
  • Copyright content used without permission can be removed or disabled. There's a counter-notice process if you think a takedown was a mistake.
  • Counterfeit items can be removed, and Walmart may take other measures.
  • Repeat infringers can have listings removed and their relationship with Walmart suspended or terminated.

Walmart's IP policy says "any unauthorized use of a registered trademark is prohibited" and bans counterfeits outright, with account termination possible.

Walmart intellectual property: what resellers should know

Most resellers who get IP complaints sell genuine products. The trouble is usually that the brand doesn't want unauthorized sellers on its listings, or that the seller's own listing content (photos, descriptions, a brand name used in the title) was copied from the brand. A few practical rules:

  • Keep invoices. Walmart's appeal guide mentions supplier or distributor invoices less than two months old and IP documents as supporting evidence. Distributor invoices carry more weight than retail receipts.
  • Don't create your own content for branded items. Match the existing listing instead of uploading the brand's images or a new product page.
  • Know your risky brands. Brands that enroll in Brand Portal and name authorized resellers are more likely to file claims against everyone else.
  • Answer fast and honestly. If you get a claim, respond through Seller Center, remove the offer if you can't back it up, and don't relist the same item.
  • Get legal advice for real disputes. Walmart's page itself suggests a lawyer if you have questions about IP rights. This guide isn't legal advice.

Questions

What is Walmart Brand Portal?

It's Walmart's system for brand owners to report IP infringement (copyright, trademark, patent and counterfeit) on Walmart.com and to assign Marketplace sellers roles such as acting brand owner or authorized reseller.

Does Walmart have a brand registry like Amazon?

Walmart's equivalent is Brand Portal. Rights owners with an active registered trademark enroll, then use it to file IP claims and manage which sellers have brand privileges.

Can a reseller join Walmart Brand Portal?

Not directly. Walmart says third-party sellers can't apply; the rights owner can make a seller an authorized reseller or acting brand owner from inside Brand Portal.

Do I need a registered trademark for Walmart Brand Portal?

Yes. Walmart requires an active registration with a seven-digit registration number. A pending application with an eight-digit serial number may not be eligible yet.

What happens when someone files an IP complaint on Walmart?

For trademark and patent claims, Walmart forwards the claim to the seller before acting and may remove infringing content. Counterfeit items can be removed. Repeat infringers can have listings removed and their accounts suspended or terminated.
