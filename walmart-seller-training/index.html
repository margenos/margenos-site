The Walmart Seller Academy is Walmart's own training for Marketplace sellers. It lives on Walmart Marketplace Learn (marketplacelearn.walmart.com), next to Walmart's seller guides, events, news and the Seller Forum. Walmart's support resources guide describes Seller Academy as "a full collection of educational video tutorials and self-paced courses." Walmart lists no price for these courses on the Academy catalog, and the guides and video pages open without a seller account. Some parts, like the Seller Forum, ask you to log in. What is Walmart Marketplace Learn?

Marketplace Learn is Walmart's hub for seller education. The site has sections for Academy, Guides, Events, Forum, Tools and News. Walmart built it to bring its seller help content into one place you can search. What you'll find:

  • Seller Academy: certification courses and short video tutorials.
  • Guides: written, step-by-step articles on every part of selling, from onboarding to taxes. Most of Walmart's policy pages are guides.
  • Events: live online sessions, including bootcamps that pair with some certifications.
  • Seller Forum: conversations with other sellers and Walmart staff, after you log in.

Is there a Walmart Seller Center tutorial?

Yes, inside Seller Center itself. Walmart's support guide lists Quick Learn, a library of "announcements, training videos and walkthroughs to help you navigate Seller Center." It also lists Marty, Walmart's AI assistant for finding answers, and a global search bar that pulls up Marketplace Learn guides as you work. Free Walmart seller training beyond the Academy

  • Walmart Marketplace on YouTube. Walmart's seller site, marketplace.walmart.com, links to its official YouTube channel.
  • The Sell Better Blog and newsletter. Walmart's support guide lists the Sell Better Blog for best practices and case studies, and a monthly Marketplace Newsletter with an archive.
  • Release Notes. Policy and product updates; you're opted in when you open a Seller Center account.
  • Walmart Connect Academy. For ads, Walmart Connect runs its own Ad Certification program, including a Sponsored Products certification. See Walmart Sponsored Products.

Plenty of independent sellers post Walmart tutorials on YouTube too. Useful ones show their Seller Center screens and cite Walmart's rules. Treat any that don't with care, because Walmart's fees and policies change.

What should a beginner learn first?

  1. Requirements and onboarding. What Walmart asks for before approval. Start with how to sell on Walmart.
  2. Fees. Referral fees by category, and WFS fees if Walmart ships for you. See Walmart seller fees and WFS fees.
  3. Listing. How offers attach to existing items and how new items are set up. See Walmart product listing.
  4. Fulfillment and policy. Shipping methods, WFS, returns and the performance standards that keep your account healthy.
  5. Sourcing. What to buy, at what cost. Walmart's courses barely touch this, and it's where most new sellers lose money.

Are paid Walmart courses worth it?

Some are. A good paid course saves you time with sourcing methods, supplier contacts or a coach who reviews your numbers. But a lot of what paid courses sell is in Walmart's own guides and Academy. Before you pay, check whether the course teaches anything Walmart doesn't already publish.

How to spot a Walmart course scam

The FTC's alert on business coaching scams (May 2026) warns about promises of "guaranteed income," a "proven system to make money" and pressure to buy right away. It also says to treat testimonials with skepticism and to check complaints with your state attorney general. Red flags specific to Walmart:

  • Guaranteed earnings or "passive" stores. No one can promise Walmart sales.
  • "Done-for-you" dropshipping from other retailers. Walmart's shipping and fulfillment policy doesn't allow another retailer to ship directly to your customer. Read our Walmart dropshipping guide first.
  • Names that sound official. "Walmart seller university" or similar isn't Walmart's program unless it's on a Walmart domain. Walmart's own training is Seller Academy on Marketplace Learn.
  • Upsells you can't see in advance. Ask for the full price and refund terms in writing.

Training covers the platform; you still need product research

Walmart's courses teach you how to list, ship and stay compliant. They don't tell you whether a specific product will make money. That takes numbers: the fees on that item, your profit at your buy cost, how many units sell, how many sellers already have stock, and who usually wins the Buy Box. Questions

What is the Walmart Seller Academy?

Walmart's own training for Marketplace sellers: self-paced certification courses and video tutorials on Walmart Marketplace Learn (marketplacelearn.walmart.com), covering listing, shipping, WFS, pricing, advertising and more.

Is Walmart seller training free?

Walmart lists no price for Seller Academy courses, and its guides and video tutorials open without a seller account. Some features, like the Seller Forum, need you to log in. Paid courses from other companies are separate.

Is there a Walmart Seller Center tutorial?

Yes. Inside Seller Center, Quick Learn offers training videos and walkthroughs, and Marty, Walmart's AI assistant, helps you find answers. Seller Academy also has videos on tasks like creating Shipping Templates.

Does Walmart Marketplace have a YouTube channel?

Yes. Walmart's seller site, marketplace.walmart.com, links to its official Walmart Marketplace YouTube channel.

Is there a Walmart WFS course?

Yes. Seller Academy has a "Getting started with Walmart Fulfillment Services" certification course, plus WFS video tutorials such as an intro to WFS and handling inventory receiving delays.

Are paid Walmart seller courses worth it?

Some are, if they teach what Walmart doesn't, like sourcing. Avoid courses that promise guaranteed income, push you to buy right away, or sell dropshipping from other retailers, which Walmart's shipping policy doesn't allow.

What should I learn first to sell on Walmart?

Requirements and onboarding, then fees, listing, fulfillment and policies. Then learn sourcing: how to check a product's fees, profit, sales and competition before you buy.
