What is retail arbitrage, and does it still work?

Retail and online arbitrage explained: the rules on Amazon and Walmart, whether it's worth it in 2026, and how to start.

Retail arbitrage is buying products at a discount in physical stores, often on clearance or sale, and reselling them online for more than you paid after fees. Online arbitrage is the same idea, except you buy from retail websites instead of store shelves. Both are legal in the U.S. when you sell genuine, new products, but each marketplace has its own rules about what you can sell and how you fulfill orders. This guide explains what retail arbitrage is, whether it's worth it in 2026, how to start, and which apps help.

What is retail arbitrage?

Arbitrage means profiting from a price difference between two markets. In reselling, the two markets are a store and an online marketplace like Walmart.com, Amazon or eBay. A toy on clearance for $10 in a store might sell for $25 online. If the spread is bigger than the marketplace fees, shipping and your time, you make money.

Retail arbitrage is how many sellers start, because you can begin with a few hundred dollars, a phone and a seller account. You buy items one or a few at a time, so you're not stuck with a case of something that doesn't sell.

What is online arbitrage?

Online arbitrage (OA) uses the same math, but you shop retail websites for markdowns, coupon stacks and cashback deals, and have the items shipped to you or a prep center. It's easier to scale because you don't drive store to store, but popular online deals attract more resellers, so margins can disappear faster.

Is retail arbitrage legal?

Yes. In the U.S., the "first sale" principle generally lets you resell a genuine product you bought lawfully without the brand's permission. The risks come from the details, not the idea:

  • Material differences. If what you sell differs from the brand's authorized version, such as missing parts, broken seals or no valid warranty, a brand may have a trademark claim.
  • Marketplace rules. Walmart and Amazon can restrict brands and categories, and ask for proof of where you bought items. Being legal doesn't mean a marketplace has to let you list it.
  • Store rules. Retailers can limit quantities. Walmart's own Terms of Use reserve the right to limit how many items a person buys and to refuse sales to dealers or resellers.

This is general information, not legal advice.

Is retail arbitrage allowed on Amazon?

Yes, Amazon allows sellers to resell items bought at retail, but many brands and categories are restricted. Getting approved for them often requires invoices from a manufacturer or distributor, and store receipts frequently aren't enough. Amazon's drop shipping policy also prohibits buying from another online retailer and having that retailer ship straight to your customer when you aren't shown as the seller.

Is retail arbitrage allowed on Walmart Marketplace?

Yes, with rules. Walmart's shipping and fulfillment policy says you may not buy products from another retailer and have that retailer ship the order directly to the customer, a practice the policy itself calls retail arbitrage. So you buy the item, receive it, and then ship it yourself or send it to Walmart Fulfillment Services. Items must be new unless Walmart has invited you to its Resold program, and some categories, such as beauty and personal care, now require extra documentation. See Walmart reselling rules for details.

Is retail arbitrage worth it? Does it still work in 2026?

It still works for sellers who check every item before they buy. What has changed is that more brands are restricted, more resellers use the same apps, and marketplaces ask for more documentation. The sellers who do well treat it like a numbers business:

  • Pros: low startup cost, fast cash turnover, no long-term supplier contracts, and it teaches you what sells.
  • Cons: it's hard to repeat the same deal, it takes time in stores, and restricted brands limit what you can list.

Many sellers use retail arbitrage to learn and fund their account, then add wholesale for steady, repeatable stock.

How to start retail arbitrage

  1. Pick your marketplace. Walmart Marketplace, Amazon and eBay have different fees, rules and competition. Our Walmart vs Amazon comparison can help.
  2. Set up the business side. Walmart requires a business tax ID (it doesn't accept an SSN) and a record of selling online. Many states also let resellers use a resale certificate for inventory.
  3. Install a scanner app. Choose one that shows profit after the fees of the marketplace you sell on.
  4. Scan before you buy. Check profit after fees, ROI, how fast it sells and how many sellers compete.
  5. Buy small, then scale. Buy a few units, see how they sell, then buy more of what works.
  6. Ship or send to fulfillment. Prep and ship items yourself, or send them to WFS or FBA.

Questions

What is retail arbitrage?

Retail arbitrage is buying discounted products in physical stores and reselling them online, on marketplaces like Walmart, Amazon or eBay, for a profit after fees.

What is online arbitrage?

Online arbitrage is buying discounted products from retail websites and reselling them on a marketplace for more. It uses the same profit math as retail arbitrage, without visiting stores.

Is retail arbitrage legal?

Yes. In the U.S., you can generally resell genuine products you bought lawfully. Problems come from selling items that differ from the brand's version, restricted brands, and marketplace or store rules. This isn't legal advice.

Is retail arbitrage allowed on Amazon?

Yes, but many brands and categories are restricted, and approval often requires invoices from a manufacturer or distributor. Amazon also prohibits having another online retailer ship directly to your customer when you aren't shown as the seller.

Does retail arbitrage still work in 2026?

Yes, for sellers who check profit after fees, sales speed and competition before buying. It's more competitive than it used to be and more brands are restricted, so many sellers combine it with wholesale.

Is there a free retail arbitrage app?

Can I do retail arbitrage on Walmart Marketplace?

Yes, if you're an approved seller and the items are new and not restricted. Walmart doesn't allow you to buy from another retailer and have it ship straight to your customer, so you receive the items first, then ship them or send them to WFS.
