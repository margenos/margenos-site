Walmart restricted brands and categories are products you can't list on Walmart Marketplace until Walmart approves you, or can't list at all. Walmart's policy names the pre-approval categories: books, ingestible products, topical products, Class II or restricted medical devices, fragrances, luxury brands, software, Halloween and select seasonal products, custom content, and jewelry, watches, precious metals and similar covered goods. Walmart doesn't publish a public list of individual restricted brands, so the safe habit is to test-list or confirm in Seller Center before you buy.

Restricted vs prohibited on Walmart

Walmart uses these words in specific ways:

  • Pre-approval (gated): you can sell it once Walmart approves your account for that category or brand.
  • Restricted: Walmart defines restricted products as ones that can't be sold in all U.S. states under applicable laws, such as some air guns, knives, electronics, furniture and sunscreen. Walmart generally prohibits listing them unless it lets you designate the states where they can be sold.
  • Prohibited: you can't sell it at all. Walmart's overview covers dozens of areas, from alcohol and recalled products to weapons, and products sourced from embargoed territories. See the full list in Walmart prohibited items.

If you break a category's requirements, Walmart says it will automatically unpublish the item, and repeated problems can lead to account suspension or termination.

Restricted brands on Walmart: brand rights and authorization

Beyond categories, brands themselves can be a problem. Walmart's policy says you must own the items you sell and have the licensing rights to sell any branded products. Here's how that plays out for resellers:

  • Brand Portal: brand owners with an active, USPTO-registered trademark can join Walmart's Brand Portal to file intellectual property and counterfeit claims, and to name sellers as authorized resellers or acting brand owners.
  • IP claims: Walmart passes trademark and patent claims to the seller, can remove infringing listings, and acts against repeat infringers, up to ending the seller relationship.
  • Authorization letters: for gated brands and beauty, a letter of authorization from the brand or an authorized distributor, plus invoices that match your business name and address, is the strongest proof you can have.

Receipts from a retail store or website prove you bought the item legitimately, but they don't make you an authorized reseller. Some brands actively file claims against unauthorized sellers, so a brand that looks open today can still cost you listings later.

Walmart ungating: how to get approved

Walmart doesn't publish a step-by-step ungating process on its policy pages, and the details can change. In general:

  1. Try to list the item. In Seller Center, set up the item or match an existing listing. If it's gated, you'll see an error or the item won't publish.
  2. Open a case with Seller Support. Use Help in Seller Center, name the category or brand, and ask what Walmart needs from you.
  3. Send the documents they ask for. Commonly invoices from a manufacturer or distributor, a letter of authorization, and safety or compliance documents for the category.
  4. Wait for a decision before you buy. Don't stock up on the assumption you'll be approved.

If you're still applying for an account, start with how to open a Walmart seller account, then work through Seller Center once you're in.

How to check before you buy inventory

The cheapest restriction check is the one you do before checkout. On every sourcing run:

  • Check the category. If it's on the pre-approval list above, confirm your account is approved.
  • Check the brand. Luxury, beauty and brands known for enforcing their reseller lists are the riskiest. Search for the brand plus "authorized retailer" to see if it controls its sellers.
  • Test-list one unit's worth. Try setting up the offer in Seller Center before buying a case.
  • Look at who sells it now. The Margenos seller view lists every seller on the listing with stock, and whether Walmart itself sells it. One seller (often the brand) with no third parties can be a sign the brand is controlled.
  • Check the condition rules. Walmart allows only new items unless you've been invited to its Resold program.

Then check the money. Margenos shows the fees, profit, ROI and sales estimate so you only fight for approval on products worth selling. If you're sourcing online, read online arbitrage on Walmart for the shipping and sourcing rules that go with restrictions.

Questions

What brands are restricted on Walmart?

Walmart doesn't publish a public list of individual restricted brands. Its policy names pre-approval categories, including luxury brands, fragrances, books, software and ingestible and topical products, and brand owners can enforce their rights through Walmart's Brand Portal. What categories need approval on Walmart Marketplace?

Books, ingestible products, topical products, Class II or restricted medical devices, fragrances, luxury brands, software, Halloween and select seasonal products, custom content, and jewelry, watches, precious metals and other covered goods.

How do I get ungated on Walmart?

Try to list the item in Seller Center, then open a case with Seller Support naming the brand or category and send the documents they request, such as distributor invoices or a letter of authorization. Wait for approval before buying inventory.

Do I need brand authorization to sell on Walmart?

Walmart's policy says you must own the items and have the licensing rights to sell branded products. For gated brands and beauty, expect to need authorization or distributor invoices. For ungated brands, keep your purchase receipts.

Can I resell beauty products on Walmart?

Beauty and personal care is one of the most restricted areas. Trade press reported in 2025 that Walmart limited these listings to brand owners and authorized distributors, with extra documentation required for others. Check Seller Center before buying.

What happens if I list a restricted item on Walmart?

Walmart says it will automatically unpublish items that don't meet a category's requirements. Repeated violations or valid IP claims can lead to account suspension or termination.
