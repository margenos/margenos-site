Three ways to find products to sell on Walmart

Most Walmart resellers use one of three sourcing methods, and many mix them. Each trades effort, cash and repeatability differently.

Retail and clearance

Clearance aisles at big-box stores, drugstores and home stores are where most resellers start. Scan anything discounted and compare it to the Walmart.com listing. The finds are often one-offs, but you learn quickly what sells. A phone barcode scanner turns a slow aisle walk into a fast one; see the Walmart scanner app and how to resell Walmart clearance.

Online arbitrage

Online arbitrage is the same idea from a laptop: watch retail sites for sales, coupons and cashback stacks, then check whether the item is profitable on Walmart. You can check far more products per hour, but so can everyone else. A browser extension that shows Walmart numbers on the listing saves the copy-paste. See online arbitrage for Walmart.

Wholesale

Wholesale means buying by the case from a brand or its authorized distributors. You get repeatable stock and invoices that prove where the goods came from, which matters if a brand or Walmart asks. Margins are usually thinner, so the numbers have to be checked carefully. See Walmart wholesale.

What to sell on Walmart Marketplace: what to look for

Whatever the source, profitable products to sell on Walmart tend to share a few traits:

  • New condition. Walmart's resold products policy prohibits items that aren't new unless you've been invited to its Resold program. Used and open-box items are out for most sellers.
  • An existing listing with steady demand. Selling on a listing that already moves is easier than creating demand. Look for steady sales, not one spike.
  • Room after fees. The price has to cover the referral fee, fulfillment, prep and your cost, with profit left over. See Walmart seller fees and WFS fees.
  • Few sellers, stock that moves. A handful of sellers whose stock sells down beats a crowd sitting on months of inventory.
  • Walmart not on the listing, or at least not at a price you can't match.
  • Small and light if you plan to use WFS, since fulfillment fees rise with weight and size.
  • Open to you. Not prohibited, and not in a category or brand that needs approval you don't have.

Red flags: products to pass on

  • Walmart sells it cheaply. When Walmart is on the listing at a low price, winning the Buy Box usually gets much harder.
  • Months of stock already listed. Add up the sellers' stock and compare it to monthly sales.
  • Today's price is a spike. If the item usually sells for less, your profit math is wrong. See Walmart product history.
  • A price war. Frequent small drops mean sellers are undercutting each other.
  • Approval categories. Walmart requires pre-approval for categories including books, ingestible and topical products, fragrances, luxury brands, software and custom content. See Walmart restricted brands.
  • No proof of purchase. Keep receipts and invoices; you must own what you sell and have the right to sell branded goods.
  • A plan to dropship from another retailer. Walmart's packaging policy doesn't allow buying from another retailer and having it ship straight to your Walmart customer.

How to check each product with numbers

Here's one product run through the checks. The numbers are made up to show the method, not real Walmart data.

  1. Profit. The item sells for $29.99 on Walmart. You look up the referral fee for its category and the WFS fee for its weight; say together they come to $9.00. Prep is $1.00. That leaves $19.99 before your cost. Buy at $12.00 and profit is $7.99, a 67% ROI.
  2. Max buy cost. If you want at least a 30% ROI, the most you can pay is $19.99 ÷ 1.30, about $15.38. Anything above that, pass.
  3. Sales. Suppose an estimate says about 60 sales a month. Ask where the estimate came from; if a tool says "not enough data", treat demand as unproven. See the Walmart sales estimator.
  4. Your share. Four sellers rotate the Buy Box at similar prices, so expect roughly 15 sales a month for you, if you match their price.
  5. Stock. The four sellers hold 300 units between them, five months of sales. Your units would wait in line. This one fails, even with good profit. Try a small test buy or pass.
  6. Price history and restrictions. For items that pass the stock check, confirm the price is stable and that you can list the item in Seller Center.

The math is the same whether you do it on paper, in our Walmart profit calculator or in a tool. More detail in how to calculate Walmart profit.

A simple weekly sourcing routine

  1. Choose one method for the week. A store route, a list of retail sites, or a supplier price list.
  2. Shortlist fast. Scan or open everything that might work and keep only items with profit at today's price.
  3. Run the full checks on the shortlist: sales, sellers and stock, Buy Box, history, restrictions.
  4. Buy small. Test two to five units of anything new before buying deeper.
  5. Track what you bought. Watch price and seller changes so you can react before your margin disappears.

Questions

How do I find products to sell on Walmart?

Pick a sourcing method (retail and clearance, online arbitrage or wholesale), shortlist items that already sell on Walmart.com, then check each for profit after fees, sales, sellers and stock, the Buy Box, price history and restrictions. Buy small quantities of what passes.

Is there a Walmart product finder?

Walmart doesn't offer a public sales rank or product finder for sellers. Tools like Margenos show fees, profit, sales estimates, competing sellers and Buy Box history on any Walmart.com listing or barcode scan, which speeds up each check. Margenos makes this page.

What are the most profitable products to sell on Walmart?

There's no fixed list. Profitable products are new-condition items with steady demand, room for profit after fees, few sellers whose stock moves, and no low-priced Walmart offer on the listing. See our guide to what sells best on Walmart for categories.

Can I sell used or open-box items on Walmart Marketplace?

Generally no. Walmart's resold products policy prohibits items not in new condition unless you've been invited to its Resold program.

Which products need approval to sell on Walmart?

Walmart's prohibited products policy lists pre-approval categories including books, ingestible products, topical products, fragrances, luxury brands, software and custom content. Check Seller Center before you buy.

How much profit should a product make on Walmart?

It's your call, but many resellers look for a few dollars of profit per unit and around a 30% return on cost, so there's room if the price drops. Work out your max buy cost before you pay.

Is wholesale better than retail arbitrage for Walmart?

Wholesale gives repeatable stock and invoices; retail arbitrage is cheaper to start and teaches you faster. Many sellers start with retail or online arbitrage and add wholesale as they grow.
