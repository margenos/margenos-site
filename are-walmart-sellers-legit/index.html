How to tell who's selling

On a Walmart.com product page, look near the price for "Sold and shipped by." If it names Walmart, it's a first-party item. If it names another business, that's a Marketplace seller. "Sold by [seller], shipped by Walmart" means the seller uses Walmart Fulfillment Services, so Walmart stores and ships it. Our Walmart third-party sellers guide covers the differences.

What Walmart requires of every seller

  • Business verification. Sellers must be registered businesses with a Business Tax ID; Walmart doesn't accept Social Security numbers. See Walmart seller verification.
  • Performance standards. Walmart's seller performance standards guide requires a return rate of 6% or below, negative feedback of 2% or below, item-not-received of 2% or below, and replies to customers within 48 hours at least 95% of the time. Missing them can lead to suspension or termination.
  • A minimum return policy. Sellers must support a return window of at least 30 days, starting seven days after the ship date, with exceptions for some items such as major appliances and luxury goods.

Your protections as a buyer

  • No return shipping charges. Walmart's policy says sellers "may not apply return shipping charges to customers."
  • In-store returns for many items. Walmart accepts eligible Marketplace items at its stores on the seller's behalf. Freight, oversized, heavy, luxury and major-appliance items, and fully regulated hazardous materials, are excluded.
  • Holiday returns. Most items bought October 1–December 31 are returnable until January 31, with exceptions such as wireless phones, major appliances and food.
  • Walmart can step in. Walmart "reserves the right to issue a refund to the customer on your behalf" when a seller doesn't follow the policy or doesn't resolve a return in time.

Quick checks before you buy

  1. Read the seller name and open its seller page for ratings and its return policy.
  2. Compare the price. A name-brand item far below every other offer is a warning sign.
  3. Look at other sellers. Many listings have several offers; "Sold and shipped by Walmart" or a WFS offer may cost about the same.
  4. Check shipping origin and time. Long delivery estimates can mean the item ships from overseas.
  5. Pay on Walmart.com only. Never pay a seller off-site. Our Walmart email scams guide shows common tricks.

Questions

Are Walmart third-party sellers legit?

Most are. Walmart requires every Marketplace seller to pass business verification, meet performance standards on returns and feedback, and offer at least a 30-day return window. Still, check the seller name, ratings and price before you buy.

How do I know if an item is sold by Walmart or a third party?

Look for "Sold and shipped by" near the price. If it names Walmart, it's first-party. Any other name is a Marketplace seller; "shipped by Walmart" means the seller uses Walmart Fulfillment Services.

Can I return a third-party Walmart item to a store?

Often, yes. Walmart accepts eligible Marketplace items at stores on the seller's behalf. Freight, oversized, heavy, luxury, major-appliance and fully regulated hazardous items are excluded.

Do Walmart Marketplace sellers charge for return shipping?

No. Walmart's returns policy says sellers may not apply return shipping charges to customers.

What if a Walmart seller won't refund me?

Contact Walmart customer care. Walmart reserves the right to refund customers on a seller's behalf when the seller doesn't follow the returns policy or resolve the request in time.
