Walmart tax exempt for resellers: what to know

How the Walmart Tax-Exemption Program works, what it says (and doesn't) about resale, and how resale certificates fit into retail arbitrage.

Walmart runs a Walmart Tax-Exemption Program (WTEP) through Walmart Business. Its Walmart Business tax-exempt page says eligible organizations can buy without sales tax online and in stores, and that eligibility "depends on state law." The page names schools, government agencies, religious institutions and charities as common examples. It does not mention resale or resale certificates, so whether a reseller can use it to buy inventory tax-free isn't something Walmart's page confirms. Ask Walmart Business before you count on it.

This is general information, not tax advice. Resale certificates are state rules. Check with your state revenue department or a tax professional.

How the Walmart Tax-Exemption Program works

From Walmart Business's tax-exempt page:

  1. Create a Walmart Business account with your name, a business email and a password, then add your organization's details.
  2. Enroll. Select "Enroll today" and provide your organization type, the state where you do business and a digital copy of your tax-exemption certificate or license.
  3. Wait for review. If Walmart approves it, tax-exempt status is applied to your account.
  4. Shop. Online, checkout removes sales tax on eligible items. In stores, use Walmart Pay in the Walmart Business app, or show a tax-exempt ID card at the register. Self-checkout lanes prompt you to apply it.

Walmart's page adds that "restrictions apply," eligible items "may vary based on state," and store-only tax-exempt credentials don't carry over online.

Resale certificates in plain terms

Most states let a registered seller buy goods for resale without paying sales tax, by giving the seller a resale certificate. The tax is then collected when the item is sold to the final customer. A few general points:

  • You usually need a sales tax permit from your state first.
  • It covers items you'll resell, not supplies you use in the business.
  • Each retailer decides whether and how it accepts certificates.
  • On Walmart Marketplace, Walmart collects sales tax from shoppers in the states on its list under marketplace facilitator laws. See Walmart seller taxes.

Why it matters for arbitrage profit

Sales tax is a direct hit to cost. On a $20 clearance item, 8% tax adds $1.60 to your cost before fees. On a thin deal that can be the whole profit. Before you rely on it

  • Ask Walmart Business directly whether your resale certificate qualifies.
  • Keep copies of your state permit and certificates.
  • Track purchases separately so you can show what was bought for resale.
  • Also check whether the items you buy are allowed on Walmart Marketplace: see restricted brands and is retail arbitrage legal.

Questions

Can resellers buy tax exempt at Walmart?

Walmart's Tax-Exemption Program lets eligible organizations shop without sales tax, and eligibility depends on state law. Walmart Business's page doesn't mention resale certificates, so ask Walmart Business whether yours qualifies.

How do I apply for Walmart tax exempt status?

Create a Walmart Business account, select Enroll today, and provide your organization type, the state where you do business and a digital copy of your tax-exemption certificate or license. Walmart reviews it and applies the status if approved.

Can I use Walmart tax exempt status in stores?

Yes. Use Walmart Pay in the Walmart Business app, or show a tax-exempt ID card at the register. Store-only credentials don't carry over to online shopping.

What is a resale certificate?

A state document that lets a registered seller buy goods for resale without paying sales tax. You usually need a state sales tax permit first, and each retailer decides whether it accepts certificates.

Does sales tax affect arbitrage profit?

Yes. Tax you pay at purchase adds to your cost. On a $20 item, 8% tax adds $1.60, which can erase a thin margin. Include it when you calculate profit.
